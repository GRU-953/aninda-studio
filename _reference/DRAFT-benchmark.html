Where this kit meets Apple and Google, and where it doesn't.

Nineteen criteria drawn from Apple's current Human Interface Guidelines, Google's Material 3 Expressive, and the parent-brand architecture Meta operates in public. Each one says what their standard is, where Aninda Studio actually stands, and nothing else. Six are outright gaps. Two of them are things the old GRU953 kit had baked in wrongly.

Apple — Human Interface Guidelines

Apple's icon guidance changed materially with the Liquid Glass release: the system now owns the corner radius and composites the icon in four appearance variants. Any kit that ships a pre-rounded, single-colourway tile is now describing a platform that no longer exists.

Google — Material 3 Expressive

Material is the harder comparison, because it is a full product design system and this is a studio identity. The criteria below are the ones that would actually bite if a product were built on these tokens for Android.

CriterionTheir standardWhere this kit standsVerdict
Tonal surfacesSeven tone-based surface containers replace opacity layers: surfaceContainerLowest through Highest.Three surfaces: ground, raised, sunken. Enough for flat design, short of Material’s depth vocabulary if this system ever ships an Android app.Gap
Deprecated role namesbackground and onBackground are deprecated in favour of surface and onSurface.The old kit named its root role bg. Carrying that name forward would import a role Google has retired.Partial
Colour rolesPrimary, secondary and tertiary key colours, each with container and on- pairs.Teal primary, four semantic hues, no secondary or tertiary. Deliberate under a two-colour brand, but it means no Material Theme Builder mapping without inventing one.Partial
Shape as tokensCorner radii as a token scale, with shape morphing and 35 shapes in the library.Four fixed radii. Simpler and defensible for a studio brand; not a shape system.Partial
Dynamic colourApps may follow the user’s wallpaper-derived palette or hold brand colours.No stated position. A bilingual Android product built on this system will hit the question on day one.Gap
MotionA motion physics system with springs, introduced in M3 Expressive.Two durations, two cubic-bézier curves, nothing over 300ms. Deliberately simpler. Defensible, but it should be written as a choice rather than an omission.Partial
Research baseM3 Expressive drew on 46 global studies with over 18,000 participants.Zero studies. One person’s judgement, stated as such. No amount of rigour in the token pipeline substitutes for this.Gap

Meta — parent-brand architecture

Not a design system comparison. Meta is here because it is the clearest public example of the structural problem this brand now has: a parent identity over products that carry their own names. These are observations of a visible structure, not citations of a published spec.

What this benchmark does not claim

  • It does not claim parity. Apple and Google run design systems with research budgets and dedicated teams; this is one person's kit, and four "meets" out of nineteen is the honest score, not a disappointing one.
  • It compares against published guidance, not against how well those companies follow their own guidance.
  • The Meta section is structural observation. No published Meta brand specification was consulted, and it is marked as such rather than dressed up as sourced.
  • Nothing here has been tested with a screen reader by a person who uses one. Contrast is computed; lived accessibility is a different claim and this kit does not make it.
  • Guidance moves. Apple's icon rules changed inside a year and invalidated a token the old kit treated as permanent. Anything dated after this file supersedes it.
