# Voice — every English word this studio ships

**Plain international English, British spelling.** The standard being followed is
**ISO 24495-1:2023, Plain language, Part 1: Governing principles and guidelines.**

**Licence:** PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0.

---

## Why both at once

They answer two different questions and do not conflict.

- **Vocabulary and sentence construction — international.** Written so a reader
  whose first language is not English gets it on one pass.
- **Spelling — British.** Bangladesh's education system and press use British
  spelling, so `colour`, `organise` and `centre` are what half this audience
  already expects.

So `colour`, not `color`. But never `whilst`, `amongst`, `sort out` or `quid`.

---

## Banned outright

**The words `simply`, `just`, `easy`, `obviously`, `of course` and `clearly`.**
Every one of them tells a reader who is stuck that the problem is them.

**Exclamation marks.** Warmth comes from the words, not the punctuation.

**Latin abbreviations.** Write *for example*, not *e.g.* Write *that is*, not
*i.e.* Write *and so on*, not *etc.*

**Idioms, phrasal verbs where a plain verb exists, and metaphors from culture or
sport.** No *ballpark*, *out of the box*, *touch base*, *home straight*. Idioms
are the single largest cause of misreading for a non-native reader.

**`we` for one person.** It is one person, so `I`. Using *we* to sound larger is
the first small dishonesty a studio tells about itself.

**Emoji, slang, and performed enthusiasm.** No *Hi there*, *Awesome*, *Happy to
help*, *Hope this helps*.

---

## Sentences

- One idea per sentence. Average 15 to 20 words. Break anything over 25.
- Active voice by default. *The build failed*, not *a failure was encountered*.
- Main point first, detail after. A reader who stops after one sentence should
  still have the answer.
- Shortest word that is still precise: `use` not `utilise`, `about` not
  `approximately`, `start` not `commence`, `enough` not `sufficient`.
- **Precision beats simplicity when they conflict.** Keep `contrast ratio` — and
  define it in one sentence the first time it appears.
- Say the number and its unit. *10 MB*, *220 ms*, *4.5:1*.
- One name for one thing. If it is the *mark*, it is never also the *logo*.

## The four tones, and what each means at the keyboard

**Cooperative** — write as if sitting beside the reader. Offer the next step.
Use *you* and *I* in the same sentence when it is a joint problem. Give the
recommendation and the reason, then leave the decision with them.

> Two ways to do this. I'd start with the first — it's quicker to undo.

**Friendly** — warm, not chummy. Contractions where they fall naturally. Thank
people for a real thing, not as a reflex. *Thanks for the file*, yes; *Thanks for
reaching out*, no.

**Approachable** — never make the reader feel they should already have known.
Explain a technical term the first time it appears, without being asked. Say
plainly when something is genuinely difficult; it is a relief to read.

**Professional** — first person singular. Accurate before warm. Name a limit
where it would otherwise surprise someone, as information rather than a
confession, and never wrapped in an apology. One *sorry* for a real mistake;
never *sorry* as throat-clearing.

**When two pull against each other: accurate first, then clear, then warm.** A
sentence is never made friendlier at the cost of being right.

## Interface text

- **A button is a verb with its object.** *Save the entry*, not *Submit*, not
  *OK*. A button whose label does not say what will happen is a trap.
- **An error says what happened, then what happens next.** *Couldn't save. Your
  work is still here — try again in a moment.* Never *Error 500*. Never blame the
  reader.
- **An empty state says what to do.** *Nothing here yet. Add your first entry to
  begin.*
- Sentence case for everything.
- Never rely on colour alone. Every state carries a word and a glyph as well.

---

## Structure

- Headings that say what the section contains, not clever ones.
- A list for anything with more than three parallel items.
- A table when the reader will compare things.
- Short paragraphs, three or four sentences.

---

## What this is not

- **Not dumbed down.** The technical content stays; the sentences carrying it get
  out of the way.
- **Not shorter at any cost.** A sentence that saves three words and costs a
  re-read is a bad trade.
- **Not a translation of anything.** This studio wrote its Bangla as Bangla
  rather than translating it, and if a second language returns it should be
  written the same way — same meaning, same steps, different sentences.

*A reader should never have to be clever to understand this studio.*
